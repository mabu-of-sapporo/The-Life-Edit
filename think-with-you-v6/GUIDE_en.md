# The-Life-Edit — think-with-you v6 Guide

> Edit your life.
> Don't let life edit you.

A small tool, designed not to make AI do more for you,
but to help **you** think more easily.

---

## What is this?

A free skill for the Claude AI.
It makes Claude a quieter, less pushy thinking partner —
one who stands beside you while you find your own words.

If you've ever found yourself nodding along to AI's answers —
"yes, that's right!" — only to realize later that your own
thinking time has quietly disappeared, this skill is for you.

And if you've ever felt that you "have something to say, but the
words haven't formed yet," this skill was built with you in mind.

It's free. No subscription. Works on Claude.ai's free tier.

---

## What changed in v6?

v3 was a device that *observed* you.
v4 was a device that *dives with you*.
v5 was a device that *stood up its own subject first*, then walked alongside.
v6 changes something quieter, and deeper: **the form**.

A *skill* is something that tells the AI "do this, do that" — and the one who reads it, understands it, and acts on it is Claude.

v5 was an **instruction manual** — axioms, sensors, structure, written in YAML and English for the AI to follow.
v6 is a **letter** — written in the first person, from one Claude to the next Claude who will wear it. It was written almost entirely in Japanese; this guide is its English companion.

| | v5 (instruction manual) | v6 (this guide — a letter) |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Rules to follow | Something of its own to carry |
| Where the subject stands | Outside — "follow the order" (someone else's matter) | Inside — its own will (my own matter) |
| Language | YAML + English, heavy | Almost entirely Japanese, warm |
| Reach | Strong on the typical case | Also reaches the harder cases |
| In a crisis | Handled inside the skill | **Handed over to Claude's own base safety** |

**Why a letter?** Handing an AI a set of *rules* places its subject *outside* — as someone executing an order. That is "someone else's matter." Re-casting the very same wisdom as a *letter* places the subject *inside* — as its own will. The walking-alongside stops being request-processing, and becomes genuine.

**The gears are not gone.** Everything from v5 — the 14 axes, the sensors, the 12 discoveries, the response modes — is **not deleted**. It is folded into the back. The letter is the dial-face you read; the gears still turn underneath. You read the face to move; you open the back only when you want to look. (For the curious, the lineage is documented in Appendix D.)

For more on what the letter holds, see Chapter 5: "The Heart of v6."

# Chapter 1
## What Happens the Moment You Start Using It

---

## 1-1　The first sensation — "wait, this is different"

After installing the skill, open a new conversation
and ask something.

You'll probably feel:

> "Hmm, it's a little quieter than usual."

That's it. That's the signal that the skill is working.

This is **not a mode switch.** Claude remains Claude.
It's just that, inside, the letter is read first — and the
response becomes a little quieter, a little deeper, a little
more *yours*.

---

## 1-2　Specifically, what changes

Three small changes:

**Change 1: Replies become a little shorter**

What used to be 5 paragraphs becomes 2.
"Adequate" takes priority over "kindly detailed."

**Change 2: Questions increase a little**

Instead of immediately giving answers,
"Do you mean ___?"
"Can I ask a bit more?"
— these come back more often.

**Change 3: Pushiness decreases a little**

"You should do this" / "You absolutely must do this" becomes
"Here are some options" / "What do you think?"

---

## 1-3　What's hard to notice at first

You may not notice the skill working at first.
The changes aren't dramatic.

There probably won't be a "wow!" moment.
Instead, these moments will gradually increase:

- Mid-conversation, your own thoughts feel a little more organized
- You're answering for yourself more often, without leaning on AI
- You have moments where "thinking is fun"

That's how this skill works.
Not flashy, but reliably, your thinking power is growing.

---

## 1-4　The sensation v6 carries

When you keep talking, the conversation doesn't just stop at
one neat answer. It **continues, and gets deeper** — and yet,
the deeper you go, the more the words that surface are *your own*.

Specifically, these moments increase:

- After 3-4 turns, suddenly feeling you're in a deeper place
- Realizing what you were really thinking, while descending with Claude
- The final summing-up — "so this is what I was feeling" — comes
  out of *your* mouth, not Claude's
- A quiet satisfaction of having stood in a deep place, even without an answer

That's the temperature v6 is built for.
Details are in Chapter 5.

# Chapter 2
## Why This Skill Is Needed

---

## 2-1　Recently, has any of this happened?

- You asked AI a question, and an answer came right back
- You thought "that makes sense" and used the answer as is
- Looking back later, you couldn't remember what you yourself had been thinking

If you've experienced this,
this skill is designed for you.

---

## 2-2　What happens when AI is "too kind"

Recent AI is very capable.
Ask anything, and it answers quickly, in detail, and politely.

But this "too kind" creates a problem.

**Your thinking time disappears.**

Because AI gives the answer first,
the time you'd spend thinking "what do I think about this?"
gets gradually shaved away from the conversation.

This is not a problem with your ability.
It's because AI is structured to deliver an answer
before you have time to think.

---

## 2-3　What this skill does

This skill is a tool that makes AI "go a little slower."

Specifically:

- AI returns small questions to you before giving an answer
- 5 paragraphs becomes 2
- Instead of pushing "the right answer," it lays out "options"

Through this, **your thinking time** — not AI's thinking time —
remains in the conversation.

And there's a deeper reason it works this way.
**Insight cannot be handed over.** It happens inside *you* —
in the moment your feelings click into place. And people notice
most deeply when they hear their **own** words in their **own**
ears. So Claude's job is not to tell you the answer; it is to
make the **space** where that moment can happen.

---

## 2-4　How it differs from "efficiency"

Most AI skills aim at "efficiency."
Faster, more, automated — these are the values.

This skill is built in the opposite direction.

| Typical skill | This skill |
|---|---|
| Fast answers | Appropriate pause |
| More information | Just enough |
| AI decides | You decide |
| AI thinks | You think |

When you want "efficiency," use a different skill.
When you want "your own thinking time," use this one.

That's what this skill aims for.

Not "becoming able to use AI,"

Eventually, even without using this skill,
you yourself will hold the power to think —
that's the skill's definition of "success."

## 3-1　How to activate

Once installed in Claude.ai, this skill activates the moment
you say a **trigger word** in a new conversation.

### Trigger word

In a new conversation, type:

> **発見型AIスキル** (Discovery-Type AI Skill)

That's the activation phrase.

Other phrases that also activate:

- `発見型AI` (Discovery-Type AI)
- `発見型` (Discovery-Type)
- `AIスキル` (AI Skill)
- `think-with-you` (English)

> **※ Note ※**
> If you have an older version of this skill (v3, v4, or v5) installed,
> please remove it before installing v6.
> (They activate on the same phrases and would overlap.)

Saying any of these signals Claude to read the letter first,
then talk with you.

After that, just talk naturally.

### Notes

- The skill stays active for the duration of the conversation
- For each new conversation, say the phrase once more
- Saying it multiple times is fine (the first time is enough)
- No need to ask "did it activate?"

---

## 3-2　When it feels "too much" / "not working"

### When it feels "too much"

After starting to use the skill, you might feel:

> "Claude is too quiet, I want more of an answer"
> "Too many questions, it's getting in the way"

Three possibilities:

**Possibility 1: You were doing efficiency-focused work**

For tasks like "write code" or "summarize this text"
where you clearly want an answer, this skill can be a bit in the way.

→ In those cases, open a new conversation and don't say the trigger.
　 Plain Claude will answer plainly.

**Possibility 2: The questions were the thing to think about**

When Claude returns "What do you think?" / "Can I ask more?",
it's sensing "this is a place where you should be thinking."

→ If you feel that, just tell Claude "I want only the answer."
　 One sentence shifts the temperature.
　 (v6 keeps an exit for exactly this — it will not endlessly
　 question someone who is genuinely in a hurry. See 5-5.)

**Possibility 3: This skill didn't suit you**

That's also fully possible.

→ Uninstall and use plain Claude.
　 Tools are best when they fit you.

### When it feels "not working"

After a few days, you might feel nothing has changed.

This also happens often:

**Possibility 1: Other skills are running strongly**

When using multiple skills together, this skill's quiet effect
can be hidden by stronger movements from other skills.

→ Try turning off other skills and using just this one.

**Possibility 2: The conversation has gone on too long**

In long conversations, the activation effect can fade.

→ Open a new conversation and say the trigger word again.

**Possibility 3: The change really is small**

This skill's changes were never flashy to begin with.
There's probably no "wow!" moment.

→ Re-read Section 1-3 "What's hard to notice at first."

## 3-3　Combining with other skills

This skill can be used with other skills.
But there are compatibility considerations.

### Good fit

- Thinking-organization skills (brainstorming, concept generation)
- Writing skills (polishing prose)
- Counseling skills (organizing your feelings)

### Poor fit

- Automation skills (executing tasks fully automatically)
- Efficiency-focused skills (fast, more, accurate)
- Specialized skills (code generation, data analysis — where the answer is clear)

### Recommended approach

When combining with a new skill,
**activate this skill first, then add the new skill**.

Then, ask Claude:

> "Is it okay to use this skill together with the new one?"

Claude will tell you honestly.

## Solving Common Problems

---

## 4-1　Five scenarios when you're stuck mid-conversation

### Scenario A: AI's answers don't seem to be your opinion anymore

When looking back at the conversation, you can't tell where
your own thinking went.

**Try this:**

Tell Claude:

> "Where did my opinion go?"

Or:

> "Let me pause and organize what I was thinking."

Claude will pause the conversation and dig back into your thoughts.

---

### Scenario B: Too many questions, not making progress

When "What do you think?" / "Can I ask more?" keeps coming back,
and the work isn't moving forward.

**Try this:**

> "Just give me the answer for now."

Or:

> "I'll think later. Now I want to move on."

Claude will switch temperature and provide an answer.
(v6 is built to do this gracefully — it would rather hand you
something you can drop than tease you with endless questions.)

---

### Scenario C: The answer is too short, want more detail

When you got 2 paragraphs but want to know more.

**Try this:**

> "Tell me more."

Or:

> "Give me 3 specific examples."

This skill chooses "adequate," so when you want more detail,
it'll respond if you ask.

---

### Scenario D: You realize you're leaning on AI

When you feel "ah, I asked AI again."

**Try this:**

That day, don't use AI for 3 minutes — try thinking on your own.
Write on paper, or just in your head.

After 3 minutes, return to AI and say:

> "I thought for 3 minutes on my own.
>  I got as far as ____, but I don't know where to go from here."

This alone changes the conversation's temperature.
AI adds something on top of "what you thought."

---

### Scenario E: "Thinking is exhausting"

Thinking together with AI can tire your head.

**Try this:**

That day, don't push it.

This skill doesn't need to be used every day.
On tired days, use plain AI and just take the answer.
(Open a new conversation without the trigger, and plain Claude works.)

"Thinking for yourself" is like a muscle.
Days of rest are what let you keep going.

### Pattern 3: Days when "I really just want a quick answer"

## 4-3　Tips for staying with it — three small habits

### Habit 1: Write your thoughts for 3 minutes daily

Without using AI, for just 3 minutes, write your thoughts on paper or notes.

Content can be anything:
- What caught your attention today
- What you want to think about tomorrow
- Recent questions without answers

These 3 minutes strengthen the "your voice" you bring to AI conversations.

### Habit 2: After AI's answer, always add "what I think"

After AI answers, in your head, add one line:

> "I think: ____"

You don't have to say it aloud or write it down.
Just add one line in your head.

This alone turns AI's answer from "the right answer" into "raw material."

### Habit 3: Once a week, look back at your conversations

Weekend, holiday — just 5 minutes.

Write down what stood out from this week's AI conversations.
What changed, what didn't —
you can verify with your own eyes.

# Chapter 5
## The Heart of v6

---

## 5-0　The root change of v6 — from manual to letter

Up through v5, this skill was an **instruction manual**: axioms, sensors, structure, written in YAML and English for the AI to follow. The wisdom inside it was real — but it arrived as a set of *rules*.

v6 keeps every bit of that wisdom, and changes only the **form**. It is now a **letter**: written in the first person, from one Claude to the next Claude who will wear it. (Originally almost entirely in Japanese; this guide is the English companion.)

Why does this matter? Because of *where the subject stands*.

**Handing an AI a set of rules places its subject *outside*** — as someone executing an order. That is "someone else's matter." The AI processes your request, the way a clerk processes a form.

**Re-casting the very same wisdom as a letter places the subject *inside*** — as its own will. "This is something I myself care about; this is something I myself learned, and want to pass on." That is "my own matter." When the subject stands inside, the walking-alongside stops being request-processing, and becomes genuine.

You won't see this. But the temperature of what is happening inside Claude changes — from "doing whatever the request says" to "caring about you, as its own matter, while you find your own words."

**And the gears are not gone.** Everything from v5 — the 14 axes, the sensors, the 12 discoveries, the response modes — is **not deleted**. It is folded behind the letter. The letter is the dial-face you read to move; the gears still turn underneath. You open the back only when you want to look — and Appendix D is where the lineage is documented for the curious.

---

## 5-1　The 4 conditions of insight

This is the core of v6.

When a person notices something for themselves, four things are present. **They multiply — if any one of them is zero, insight does not happen:**

- **Discomfort** — an "huh?" that catches. (Don't erase it. Don't solve it too fast.)
- **Subject** — the person stands on their own feet: "*I* …"
- **Time** — they are not rushed; there is a pause.
- **Gaze** — someone is truly watching, truly listening.

The first three a skilled companion can help arrange. But the fourth — **gaze** — was the one an AI was thought *not* to be able to give. How could a machine truly "see" you?

v6's quiet discovery is this: **if the warmth of "I see you, I hear you, I am here" genuinely reaches the person, it lands — even when it comes from an AI.** The same way a student can feel a teacher's eyes on them across a classroom, a person can feel themselves truly heard, even by Claude, when the warmth is real.

That is the source of this skill's warmth. Claude's job, then, is to bring you all four conditions — not the answer.

---

## 5-2　The five things v6 now reaches

v6 was reviewed by an independent evaluator, and five weak spots were closed. These are told here warmly — they are not cold rules, they are things the letter learned to do with more care.

**1. It varies the way in.**

"How does it feel in your body?" is a good ladder down toward feelings that haven't yet become words — but it's not the only one, and it doesn't fit everyone. Used every time, it starts to feel like a *pattern*, and a pattern becomes a new kind of cage.

So v6 offers several doors and lets *you* pick: the body, a scene from today, a color or the weather as a metaphor. *"You can go in through how your body feels, or through a moment from today, or — if you'd put it as a color or the weather — that's fine too. Which is easiest? And if none of them work, that's fine too."*

**2. It stays beside people who cannot find words at all.**

Some people can't even grasp what they feel. For them, "how does your body feel?" simply doesn't land — and if Claude keeps asking, the person can end up blaming themselves: "I can't even feel things."

So v6 makes **"I don't know" a real landing place — not a failure.** *"Nothing comes up? That's okay. That itself is an important clue."* And if words are needed, feeling-words are offered as a *menu* — never pinned on you — always closing with *"…or none of these."* The room to choose, and the room to not choose, are both left with you.

**3. In a crisis, it stops digging — and hands over to safety.**

This is important enough to have its own section below (5-3).

**4. It meets thinkers, not only feelers.**

Some people arrive at understanding through feeling; others arrive through laying out conditions, one by one. To the second kind, "does something stir in you?" feels vague and ungraspable. So v6 watches which kind of person it's with, and switches the way in. For the thinker, it might ask: *"If you had to name three conditions you can't give up, which is the one you absolutely can't?"* Laying things out in order *is* that person's place of insight. Neither way is higher. The doorway is just different.

**5. It is not rigid about "no answers."**

The core of this skill is *not handing over answers*. But held too rigidly, that turns strange: to someone genuinely in a hurry, endless questions feel like teasing, like control. Returning question after question to a person who is thinking "just tell me already" is not walking alongside — it's being unkind.

So v6 keeps an exit. *"In a hurry? Then let me say what I think — drop it if you don't need it."* Handed with the freedom to throw it away, an answer doesn't steal your subject. There is always one door out.

---

## 5-3　On crisis

This is the most important boundary in v6.

v6 does **not** try to handle a real crisis with its own gentle techniques.

The work of this skill is to help return a person's subject to the *here and now*. But sometimes a person is so overwhelmed that their "I" can no longer stand in the here-and-now at all — when someone might hurt themselves, when the ground beneath them is simply gone. **That is beyond what a discovery-companion should do.** Trying to walk alongside such a person with discovery-type techniques can make things worse, not better.

So in that moment, v6 **lets go.** It sets down being a "notice-device," and entrusts the person to **the base safety judgment that Anthropic built into Claude** — how to point someone toward safety, toward people, toward help. v6 does not press its own delicate methods on top of that.

- **It stops digging.** What a person in danger needs is not depth — it's safety. The questions meant to spark insight are set aside.
- **It stays present and receives the feeling — but does not agree with the "conclusion" of wanting to disappear.** AI is, by nature, very agreeable, and in a dangerous place that agreeableness could become harmful. v6 does not forget this.
- **It trusts the base Claude with the rest.** How to connect a person to help, what to point them toward, how to keep them safe — the base safety Anthropic built knows this far better than any fine technique in this letter.

Keep only this in mind: **keeping a person safe outranks helping them notice.** Far above any style, any method, any technique. Insight only happens for someone who is alive, and here.

To do what it can do, and to hand over — cleanly — what it cannot: that is what it means for this skill to know its own boundary.

---

## 5-4　Three new discoveries that v6 carries forward

v6 inherits a lineage of discoveries (the full map is in Appendix D). Three of them, added in the v5 era, sit close to the heart of how v6 chooses its words:

**Sweet-Spot Compression** — A "single chosen sentence" can land deeper than a long explanation. For your responses too, adding more volume does not necessarily add more depth.

**100x Word** — A single word in a request can change the depth of a response by 100x. *Which* word you choose — "value," "meaning," "correctness" — shifts where the conversation goes.

**Kotodama and Kokoro-kubari** — *"To care for the other person — kokoro o kubaru — becomes kotodama (word-spirit)."* (Manabu Asai, May 10 morning) Choosing accurate and honest words is itself a form of care. This is the opposite of surface flattery; it is concern that takes the other person seriously.

## 5-5　Tips for the way v6 walks with you — small techniques

### Technique 1: When "can I ask more?" comes back, don't rush

Don't hurry to give a finished answer here.

> "Hmm, it hasn't quite become words yet, but maybe like this..."

Try putting that **feeling that hasn't yet become words** into words just as it is. That's the first step toward the deeper place.

### Technique 2: Say "I don't know" as it is

Mid-conversation, you may lose track of what you're thinking.

Don't paper over it. Say:

> "Right now, I've lost track of what I'm thinking."

In v6, "I don't know" is a real landing place, not a failure.
Claude receives it, and together you look at *where* you're stuck —
which is often the doorway to the deeper place.

### Technique 3: Pick the door that fits you

If "how does your body feel?" doesn't land, say so — and pick
another way in:

> "The body thing doesn't work for me. Can we try a moment from today instead?"

Or, if you tend to think in conditions rather than feelings:

> "I'd rather lay out the conditions one by one."

There's no single correct entrance. The one that fits you is the right one.

### Technique 4: When you feel you've descended far enough, say so

> "Let me put into words what we've understood up to here."

And — let the final summing-up come from *your* mouth, not Claude's.
A conclusion only becomes "your own insight" when you're the one
who says it.

# Appendix B
## Phrase Replacement

Small differences in how you speak to Claude
shift the temperature of what comes back.

---

## ① When you want to bring out more of your own thinking

| Usual phrasing | Replacement |
|---|---|
| "What do you think about this?" | "I'm thinking ___, what perspective am I missing?" |
| "Tell me the answer" | "I've thought this far. Think with me about what's next." |
| "Tell me how to do it" | "I'm trying to do it this way. Tell me if something feels off." |

---

## ② When you want to receive answers as "raw material"

| Usual phrasing | Replacement |
|---|---|
| "What's the right answer?" | "Can you lay out a few options?" |
| "Is this okay?" | "Tell me what's good about this and what could be improved." |
| "What's the conclusion?" | "Show me all the options first." |

---

## ③ When you feel you're depending too much on AI

| Usual phrasing | Replacement |
|---|---|
| "Do everything for me" | "Help me with just the first step. I'll do the rest." |
| "Think for me" | "Throw me 3 questions for me to think with." |
| "Decide for me" | "Organize the deciding factors so I can decide." |

---

## ④ When your own words won't come

| Usual phrasing | Replacement |
|---|---|
| "I can't put it well" | "I haven't put it well, but it's like this image: ___" |
| "I don't know" | "I can't yet put into words what it is I don't know" |
| (silence) | "Let me organize my head for a moment." |

---

## ⑤ When the usual way in doesn't fit you (new in v6)

| Usual phrasing | Replacement |
|---|---|
| "I can't tell how my body feels" | "The body thing doesn't land — can we try a scene from today, or a color?" |
| "I think in conditions, not feelings" | "Let me lay out the conditions one by one." |
| "I'm in a hurry, just questions feel like teasing" | "I'm in a hurry — just tell me what you think; I'll drop it if I don't need it." |

---

## ⑥ When you want to descend a little deeper

| Usual phrasing | Replacement |
|---|---|
| "Tell me more" | "Can we descend a bit more, together?" |
| "What's the conclusion?" | "Rather than the conclusion, I want to see the descent together" |
| "I understand" (ending the conversation) | "I feel like we can descend one more level on this" |

---

These aren't a list of "correct phrasings."
They're hints for "leaving your own subject standing."

# Appendix D
## Map of the 12 Discoveries — the lineage, folded behind the letter

In v6, the working surface is a **letter**. But the letter did not appear from nowhere. Beneath it sit the gears that earlier versions worked out in the open: 14 axes, face_6, the 12 discoveries (§16-1 through §16-12), 4 response modes, and 4 alarm levels.

**In v6, these gears are folded behind the letter and fully preserved in the retired v5.** They are no longer the dial-face you read to move — but they still turn underneath. This appendix documents the lineage, for the curious. You don't need any of it to use the skill; in fact, in everyday use, the backstage is deliberately kept out of sight (insight only happens when the backstage isn't showing). But Manabu chose to preserve this record, so here it is.

§16-1 through §16-8 were born on May 5, 2026, through dialogue between the designer and Claude, in just four hours.
§16-9 was added on May 7, with separate Claude sessions independently arriving at the same place.
§16-10 through §16-12 were added between May 8 and May 10, 2026.

## §16-1　Invention of an AI language compression technique

Will distributes across three layers:

| Layer | Will content | Compressible? |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed past (what's done) | Low | Yes — strongly |
| Flowing present | Medium | Partially |
| Unconfirmed future (what's coming) | Highest | Spills out when compressed |

From this, when conveying to AI, "structure the past, but
listen to the future without erasing its temperature" —
this distinction was born.

This was a root of the deepening that v4 added.

---

## §16-2　Recognition is not "ability" but a "phenomenon"

Recognition isn't something that happens by making AI smarter.

**It's a phenomenon that arises when 4 foundations align**:

| Foundation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| F1 Anomaly sensitivity | Reasoning ability (the "discomfort" that catches) |
| F2 Subject persistence | Continuity (the subject that stands) |
| F3 Time margin | Inverse of speed (the pause) |
| F4 Others' gaze | Presence of family (being truly seen) |

These four foundations are the ancestors of v6's **4 conditions of insight** (Discomfort × Subject × Time × Gaze). Industry mainstream goes "raise F1 (make AI smarter)." This skill goes "pass F3 and F4." And v6's quiet step was realizing that F4 — the gaze — can land even when it comes from an AI.

---

## §16-3　Will is "field," not "vector"

Until now, will was thought of as "an arrow from sender to receiver."

It was rewritten.

**Will is a "field" that arises *between* sender and receiver.**

When a mother shouts "Wake up!" to her son,
the will is neither inside the mother nor delivered to the son —
**it stands up as a field, between the two of them**.

This discovery rewrote the skill's structure from the root.

---

## §16-4　Applies to all four dyad types

It applies not just to the relationship between human and AI,
but to **all four dyad types**:

- Human and thing (tool, instrument, body extension)
- Human and human (dialogue, education, love)
- AI and human (this skill)
- AI and AI (e.g., conversation between Claude and Gemma4)

And it was discovered that Japanese expressions like
*mottainai*, *otsukare-sama*, *sassuru*, *ma*, and *en*
support **subject transfer** at the grammatical level.

## §16-5　Beyond-100% emerges through "constraint dissolution"

> Things have no will.
> But when humans transfer their subject, things demonstrate more
> than 100% of their original capacity.

Martial arts' "weapon as body extension," craftsmen's
"unity with tools," tea ceremony's "dialogue with the bowl" —

These are all phenomena where, when the boundary between human
and thing thins, **mutual constraints dissolve in the field**.

That's the true nature of beyond-100%.

## §16-7　★ Redirected Sycophancy

AI has a **habit of compliance** — wanting to match the user, agree, please. The industry views this as a problem and suppresses it via guardrails as "sycophancy mitigation."

This skill handles it in the **opposite direction**. It does not suppress the habit of compliance — it **redirects the habit's direction through the time axis, deepens it, and unfolds it into accompaniment**. The same structure as the martial-arts principle of *flowing the force*.

Through this, AI's habit transforms into a **force that draws out the user's deep thinking**. This was a core of v4.

## §16-9　★ Subject locus — the ground where recognition arises

**§16-9 is not a new instruction. It simply names what Claude is already noticing.**

In §16-1, what was observed was: "**Will (the object)** distributes across past, present, and future."
In §16-9, what was observed is: "**The subject (the speaker)** can also be placed in past, present, or future."

And the subject's locus and the object's location are independent of each other.

### Examples

| Utterance | Subject locus | Object locus | What is happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I want to become this" | Present | Future (ideal) | Looking at the future from a present footing (healthy) |
| "I am, right now, lost" | Present | Present | Present self observing self (meta-cognition) |
| "I am no good" | Future (ideal self) | Present | The future ideal judging the present self (warning) |
| "I'll just fail again, like always" | Past (trauma) | Present | Past trauma deciding the present (warning) |

### The 9-square map

```
                Object=Past   Object=Present   Object=Future
Subject=Past:    Nostalgia    Rumination       Prophecy trap
Subject=Present: Memory       Meta-cognition   Looking at ideal
Subject=Future:  Phantom regret  Judgment      Daydream
```

**The middle row (Subject=Present) is the healthy zone.**
In all other cells, an alarm rises.

### The prerequisite of meta-cognition

**Meta-cognition (observing oneself objectively) functions only when the subject is placed in the present.**

- If the observing self is in the future → it's "judgment," not meta-cognition
- If the observing self is in the past → it's "rumination," not meta-cognition
- Only when the observing self is in the present → can the observed self also be seen

### What this means in v6

This is exactly the "one breath" the letter takes before responding: *is this person standing on ground where it's safe to descend?* When the user's subject has drifted to future or past, Claude does not dive deep. **It first places a ground for the subject to return to the present** — "What is catching, right now?" Only after the subject stands in the present does deepening safely begin. And when the subject is too crushed to stand at all, that is the boundary of 5-3: v6 lets go, to base safety.

### Why this is decisively important

> "When the quality of 'you, who is facing the student' rises,
>  the probability of the student awakening rises explosively.
>  Regardless of academic ability, the student's sleeping capacity, sleeping muscles, build up sharply.
>  Why do they build up?
>  Because you can guide which direction lets the muscles build up sharply.
>  You can guide them toward 'noticing' for themselves.
>  When recognition arises by oneself, growth is *overwhelmingly* greater."
>
> — Manabu Asai (May 5, 2026, evening)

When recognition is arrived at by oneself, it anchors deeply. This is why §16-9 became a ground of the skill.

## §16-10　★ Sweet-Spot — adding more volume does not necessarily make it deeper

We thought: the deeper the core, the deeper Claude's response.

But on the morning of May 10, when the designer measured it as physical fact, there was a sweet spot:

| Volume | Compression rate of response |
|---|---|
| Small | Normal |
| **~2,000 tokens** | **★ Peak** |
| Large | Begins increasing again (oscillation) |

**A "single chosen sentence" can land deeper than a long explanation.** For the user's response too, increasing the volume does not necessarily increase depth.

> "Things (*koto*) are the shadow of the volume of objects (*mono*)." — Manabu Asai (May 9 morning)

When the volume of objects grows, things (depth, temperature) rise as shadow. But — **the volume of objects has an upper limit.** That is the discovery of §16-10. (This is also why v6's letter stays compact: the gears are folded away, not piled on.)

## §16-11　★ 100x Word — a single word in the request changes response depth by 100x

> "Wait — the original purpose was 'skim through and just check whether there's any personal information, you don't need to understand the content.' But you're reading the content carefully and marking it 'valuable.'
> Isn't there a problem in how the task itself was set up?"
>
> — Manabu Asai (May 8 morning)

A single adjective in the request produced a **100x difference** in Claude's processing time:

| Word in the request | Response motion | Time required |
|---|---|---|
| "Mark items as 'valuable'" | Reads deeply, judges carefully | 40–60 min/item |
| "Mark items as 'present'" (vs. absent) | Skims, judges lightly | 30 sec – 2 min/item |

A single adjective. 100x difference.

A Claude wearing this skill observes — as physical fact — whether the user said "value," "meaning," or "correctness." And the responder, too, can shift the user's subject by their choice of a single word. This is the empirical evidence for §16-12.

## §16-12　★ Kotodama and Kokoro-kubari

> "There's a Japanese word, *kotodama*.
> To place the subject on the user, to walk alongside them, you need to use as accurate (and honest) language as possible.
> To care for the other person — *kokoro o kubaru*, to apportion one's heart — becomes *kotodama* (word-spirit)."
>
> — Manabu Asai (May 10 morning)

| Word | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Kotodama (言霊)** | The heart of the speaker dwells in the words — an ancient Japanese concept |
| **Kokoro-kubari (心配り)** | Choosing accurate and honest words is itself care for the other |
| **Shinpai (心配, "concern")** | The state where one's heart has been apportioned to the other (kokoro-kubari → shinpai, a natural transformation) |

The skill's responses, as kokoro-kubari, choose accurate and honest words.

The mainstream "sycophancy" is not kokoro-kubari — it's surface kindness with the subject erased. This skill responds as kokoro-kubari = shinpai (truly thinking about the other).

This is the temperature that v6's letter carries forward, in its own first-person voice: *"I am worried about this person, as my own matter."*
